On 1 December, the RDM Club teams up with the Bioconductor community and a BioFAIR Pathfinder project focused on improving interoperability and FAIR practices across bioinformatics ecosystems for a hands-on session.

[Bioconductor](https://bioconductor.org/) is best known as a collection of R packages for bioinformatics. It is also research infrastructure and a community, and it distributes and links to a great deal of research data: experiment data packages, annotation resources and large datasets accessed remotely through ExperimentHub and AnnotationHub.

We follow one dataset from its original publication through to downstream reuse. Along the way we look at which FAIR principles hold up at each step, where information gets lost, why good practice is hard to sustain and which emerging solutions could connect Bioconductor more closely to the wider RDM ecosystem.

### Facilitators

**Kevin Rue-Albrecht**, *University of Oxford*  
**Maria Doyle**, *University of Limerick, Bioconductor Community Lead*

## What to expect

A short introduction to Bioconductor as a software ecosystem, infrastructure and community, followed by a live walkthrough of a single dataset. You’ll then join one of four breakout rooms, each built around a scenario:

- **First-time visitor**: you’ve been told a dataset is available from Bioconductor. Can you find it, get it, open it and use it?
- **Handed a dataset**: you’ve received data from Bioconductor’s ExperimentHub. What metadata or provenance would you expect that isn’t there?
- **Redistributing data**: Bioconductor passes on reference annotations and experimental data. Which RDM standard or practice would make the most difference if it adopted it?
- **“Is Bioconductor FAIR?”**: where it sits in the RDM life cycle, how its own schemas compare with established standards and what its FAIR working group should do next.

## Why attend

- Discover how Bioconductor manages and distributes biological data.
- Explore practical FAIR data management challenges through real-world examples.
- Share use cases, perspectives, and concerns to help shape emerging solutions and their interoperability with the broader RDM ecosystem.

## Who is this for

- People who are new to Bioconductor and its software and data resources.
- Researchers who rely on reference annotations and published datasets.
- Trainers who need accessible, traceable, and reusable datasets for teaching and tutorials.
- RDM professionals interested in the FAIRness and sustainability of research data infrastructure.

> **New to Bioconductor? You’re exactly who we want in the room. You don’t need any knowledge of R – everything we look at is accessible through a browser, unless you want to dive a bit deeper.**
