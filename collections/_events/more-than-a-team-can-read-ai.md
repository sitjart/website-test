When data outgrows what a human team can process, AI is most useful as infrastructure: turning unstructured text into structured, computable forms that support automated processing and reasoning at scale.

In this session, we'll share how the team at Leicester built that infrastructure, from schema-based extraction to knowledge graphs and machine-readable rules, and what it costs them along the way.

## Speaker

**Yunze Wang (Patrick)**
Research Software Engineer, University of Leicester

## **What to expect**

- **Structured extraction** – pulling information from supplied text into defined schemas, backed by use cases.
- **Knowledge graphs from text** – building knowledge graphs from input text and tackling challenges and solutions, including entity resolution, relationship enrichment, and indexing resolution, relationship enrichment and indexing.
- **Normative knowledge graphs** (constraints) – extraction of use conditions, contracts, requirements, etc. and scalable reasoning on the extracted format, backed by our solutions.
- **Lessons learned** – the lessons, pitfalls, costs and efficiency compromises we learned from the process.

## **Who is this for**

Teams and researchers who generate, manage or consume data in large quantities and want to explore AI-assisted automation and reasoning at scale.
