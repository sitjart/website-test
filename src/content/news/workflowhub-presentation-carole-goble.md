<p>On <strong>16 July 2025</strong>, Professor <strong><a href="https://elixiruknode.org/people/carole-goble/" data-type="people" data-id="18640">Carole Goble</a></strong>, Head of Node for ELIXIR-UK and based at the University of Manchester, delivered a presentation on <strong><a href="https://elixiruknode.org/service/workflowhub/" data-type="service" data-id="18745">WorkflowHub</a>, </strong>an ELIXIR-UK service, as part of the <strong><a href="https://workflows.community/talks" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow">Workflow Community monthly talk series</a></strong>. She was joined by <strong>Dr Johan Gustafsson</strong> (<a href="https://www.biocommons.org.au/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow">Australian BioCommons</a>, University of Melbourne).</p>

<p>Titled <em>“WorkflowHub: A registry for computational workflows”</em>, the talk explored how WorkflowHub helps make computational workflows more <strong>findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable (FAIR)</strong>. As a central registry, it connects diverse workflow ecosystems, supports sharing and reuse, and promotes workflows as <strong>citable scholarly artefacts</strong>.</p>

<p>WorkflowHub now hosts over <strong>1,200 registered workflows</strong>, representing contributions from hundreds of research organisations around the world.</p>

<p>This session was part of the <strong>Workflow Community Talks</strong>, a monthly webinar series coordinated by the <a href="https://workflows.community/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow">Workflow Community Initiative</a> to bring together developers, researchers, and infrastructure providers working on workflows across life sciences and beyond.</p>
