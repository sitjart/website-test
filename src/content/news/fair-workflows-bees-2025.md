<p>On June 30 2025, Professor <a href="https://elixiruknode.org/people/carole-goble/" data-type="people" data-id="18640">Carole Goble</a>, Head of Node of ELIXIR-UK, was invited to deliver a keynote at the <a href="https://www.lifewatch.eu/bees-2025/">LifeWatch Biodiversity and Ecosystem eScience (BEeS) Conference 2025</a>, hosted by the Hellenic Centre for Marine Research in Crete.</p>

<p>Her talk, <em>"Sharing and re-using computational workflows – WorkflowHub and FAIR Workflows in Biodiversity"</em>, explored the role of open, interoperable workflows in supporting biodiversity research and environmental monitoring. She highlighted how platforms like<a href="https://workflowhub.eu"> WorkflowHub</a> and the FAIR Workflows are helping to make complex computational analyses reusable and aligned with the FAIR principles.</p>

<p>The <a href="https://www.lifewatch.eu/bees-2025/">BEeS Conference</a> brings together European Research Infrastructures, scientists and policymakers to tackle the Triple Planetary Crisis – climate change, biodiversity loss and pollution – through data-driven innovation. The 2025 edition welcomed 193 participants and offered over 50 presentations, featuring hands-on training, poster sessions and cross-disciplinary collaboration focused on ecological responses, virtual labs and sustainability solutions.</p>

<p>Carole’s keynote underlined the importance of infrastructure-level coordination and community-driven tooling to ensure that biodiversity science benefits from robust, shareable and reproducible computational methods.</p>
