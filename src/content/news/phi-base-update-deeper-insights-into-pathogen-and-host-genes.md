<p>ELIXIR UK is pleased to announce the release of <a href="https://phi5.phi-base.org/#/home"><strong>PHI-base 5</strong> </a>in January 2025, a significant update to the Pathogen-Host Interaction database. As an ELIXIR-UK-endorsed service, PHI-base has served the scientific community for two decades, providing valuable insights into pathogen-host interactions.</p>

<p>This latest version offers deeper insights into how genes influence interactions between <strong>pathogens</strong> and their <strong>hosts</strong>. It provides standardised ontology-based data that is machine-readable and human-friendly, making it easier for researchers to integrate into their workflows.</p>

<p>PHI-base 5 supersedes the previous version (version 4), which will remain available at <a href="http://www.phi-base.org">www.phi-base.org</a> until later this year.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="key-enhancements">Key enhancements</h3>

<p>The latest version of PHI-base offers:</p>

<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Standardised ontology-based data</strong>: machine-readable and user-friendly</li>

<li><strong>One-gene, one-page design:</strong> for comprehensive gene-level summaries that streamline exploration and cross-referencing</li>

<li><strong>Expanded content:</strong> curating more detailed phenotypes, including antibiotic resistance, gene-for-gene interactions, and mutational comparisons</li>

<li><strong>Integration-ready</strong>: enhanced compatibility with biological databases such as UniProtKB and KnetMiner.</li>
</ol>

<p><strong>Kim Hammond-Kosack</strong>, lead scientist and PHI-base co-founder, says, “This latest advancement of the precision of the information we are capturing will make PHI-base even more useful for discovery research, disease control, and monitoring for changes in pathogens that are of the most concern.”</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="research-impact">Research impact</h3>

<p>PHI-base 5 has a growing focus on pathogens affecting key crops, such as wheat and rice, and plays a crucial role in advancing research within the One Health concept –&nbsp;spanning crop, animal, human, and ecosystem health.</p>

<p>By mid-2025, PHI-base is projected to surpass 10,000 curated genes, further solidifying its utility for discovery research, disease control and pathogen monitoring.</p>
