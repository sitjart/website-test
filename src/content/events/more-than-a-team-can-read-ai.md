<p>When data outgrows what a human team can process, AI is most useful as infrastructure: turning unstructured text into structured, computable forms that support automated processing and reasoning at scale.</p>

<p>In this session, we'll share how the team at Leicester built that infrastructure, from schema-based extraction to knowledge graphs and machine-readable rules, and what it costs them along the way.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="speaker">Speaker</h2>

<p><strong>Yunze Wang (Patrick)</strong><br>Research Software Engineer, University of Leicester</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-to-expect"><strong>What to expect</strong></h2>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Structured extraction</strong> – pulling information from supplied text into defined schemas, backed by use cases.</li>

<li><strong>Knowledge graphs from text</strong> – building knowledge graphs from input text and tackling challenges and solutions, including entity resolution, relationship enrichment, and indexing resolution, relationship enrichment and indexing.</li>

<li><strong>Normative knowledge graphs</strong> (constraints) – extraction of use conditions, contracts, requirements, etc. and scalable reasoning on the extracted format, backed by our solutions.</li>

<li><strong>Lessons learned</strong> – the lessons, pitfalls, costs and efficiency compromises we learned from the process.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="who-is-this-for"><strong>Who is this for</strong></h2>

<p>Teams and researchers who generate, manage or consume data in large quantities and want to explore AI-assisted automation and reasoning at scale.</p>
