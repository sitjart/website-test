<p><a href="https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/" data-type="link" data-id="https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/">RO-Crate</a> is a simple way to package research data together with the information that gives it meaning – who created it, what it relates to, how it was produced, and how it can be reused. In this session, Eli Chadwick from the University of Manchester will give a layperson-friendly introduction to RO-Crate, with a focus on what its is, why it matters, and how people can start making use of it.<a href="https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.1/introduction.html" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7XSQKJOpZ20" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a><a href="https://www.ldaca.edu.au/news/posts/open-repositories-2024-ro-crate/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>

<p>This will be an informal, accessible session for anyone working with research data in universities. You do not need to be coding-savvy to follow along, and the talk is designed for people who know about data but may not yet know how RO-Crate work in practice.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-to-expect">What to expect</h3>

<p>The session will start with a 20–30 minute talk sharing an overview of RO-Crate and its value for research data management. After that, we will open a discussion where questions from the audience are welcome.<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7XSQKJOpZ20" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="who-its-for">Who it’s for</h3>

<p>This call is aimed at data stewards, data leads, and others supporting research data in universities.<br>It should also be useful for anyone who has heard of RO-Crate but wants a clearer explanation of what it is are and how it is used in practice.</p>
