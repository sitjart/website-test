<p>When does an AI output become evidenced, documented and reviewed enough to support a real experimental action? Our next RDM Club call takes on exactly that question. </p>

<p><strong>Huayu Xin</strong> (University of Edinburgh), part of the <a href="https://aibio.ac.uk/about/working-groups/rri-and-ethics/">AIBIO-UK Responsible Research Working Group</a>, will introduce a perspective paper the group is developing on trustworthy AI in the biosciences – with particular attention to the data side of the argument.</p>

<p>The talk introduces <strong>Traceable Trust</strong>, a framework for assessing when AI-supported research decisions become reviewable and, in turn, actionable. The case rests on something RDM Club members will recognise: data provenance, metadata and documentation are not housekeeping around trustworthy AI – they are what makes it possible.</p>

<p>Huayu will ground this in three examples drawn from the paper, each at a different scale, before turning to what it all means for the records we keep day to day.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-to-expect"><strong>What to expect</strong></h2>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>An introduction to Traceable Trust as a way of deciding when an AI-supported decision is reviewable and actionable</li>

<li>Why data provenance, metadata and documentation sit at the heart of trustworthy AI in the biosciences</li>

<li>Three worked examples at different scales: OSAI at the ecosystem level, the Agentic Automation Canvas at the project level, and AI-guided DBTL laboratories at the laboratory level</li>

<li>What this means for everyday RDM: ELNs, LIMS, workflow provenance records, RO-Crate-like objects, and the documentation of failed or negative results</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="who-is-this-for"><strong>Who is this for</strong></h2>

<p>This community call is for anyone interested in research data, AI and reproducibility, open to all. You'll find it especially relevant if you:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Are a member of the ELIXIR-UK RDM Club, or curious about joining</li>

<li>Work with research data, metadata or provenance in the life sciences</li>

<li>Are exploring how to use AI or automation responsibly in a research setting</li>

<li>Work with ELNs, LIMS, workflow provenance records or RO-Crate-like objects</li>

<li>Want to think harder about how AI-supported decisions get evidenced and reviewed before anyone acts on them</li>
</ul>
